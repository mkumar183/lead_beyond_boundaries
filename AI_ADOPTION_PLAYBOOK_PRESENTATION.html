The Challenge: Why AI Adoption Is Hard

AI Adoption is not a tooling problem. It is an adaptive challenge — and it stalls for seven reasons, in three groups.

Group A · The Human Shift
  • 1. A new skill, not a tool upgradePrompt engineering is a different craft — understand systems & design, but stop writing the syntax. An identity shift.
  • 2. Speed of change creates fearModels evolve faster than people can adapt. The velocity itself breeds anxiety — no stable ground to stand on.
  • 3. Psychological safety under threatEngineers fear losing their jobs. The future of the engineer feels uncertain — and uncertainty paralyses.
Group B · The Reality Gap
  • 4. Productivity is unclear — AI hallucinatesCode is generated fast, but confidently wrong. Testing & validation still slow. Net gain is murky.
  • 5. The bottleneck shiftedCoding got faster; testing, review, deployment, observability did not. The slowest step moved.
  • 6. Hype has hidden the real workGen AI hype buries real ML (supervised/unsupervised). Engineers aren't trained on data — adopting hype before literacy.
Group C · The Responsibility Gap
  • 7. Ethical AI & security threatsBias, transparency, accountability are now engineering concerns. New attack surfaces — data leakage, prompt injection, IP exposure. Move fast and stay responsible.
"Adaptive challenges can only be addressed through changes in people's priorities, beliefs, habits, and loyalties." — Heifetz

Every one of these is adaptive — none is solved by buying a licence. The rest of this deck shows which principle resolves each.

Adaptive vs Technical: Diagnosing the Real Problem

Get the diagnosis wrong, and every subsequent move misfires.

❌ If we treat it as TECHNICAL

  • Known solution: buy licences, run training videos, set quotas
  • Command compliance: "everyone uses Copilot by Q3"
  • Measure: % logged in, lines generated, licence utilisation
  • Assume code generation = productivity (ignore the rest of the SDLC)
  • Assume engineers just need a tool, not a new skill

✅ But AI Adoption is ADAPTIVE

  • New skill, not new tool — learn to direct, not write
  • Identity under threat — the role cuts across everything
  • Fear is rational — today's workflow feels temporary
  • Productivity is ambiguous — hallucination & validation eat the gains
  • Bottleneck shifted — testing, review, deploy are now slowest
  • Hype hides the work — a data-literacy gap, not a licensing gap
  • Ethics & security — new habits in every engineer, not a bolt-on

Diagnosing this correctly is the first leadership move. This slide reframes the whole conversation from "rollout" to "transition."

How We'd Approach This Without the Training

The Loyal Soldier defaults — well-intentioned, but adaptive-blind.

  • Respond to expectations → Wait for the executive mandate; cascade as a directive → ❌ mandating into fear produces compliance theatre
  • Comply and appease → "Leadership wants AI used, so use it" → ❌ ignores the psychological-safety crisis underneath
  • Communicate at task levels → "X% adoption, Y licences provisioned" → ❌ hides that real productivity gains are still unproven
  • Wait to be invited → Each EM runs their own pilot; no shared learning → ❌ nobody addresses the SDLC bottleneck downstream
  • Treat prompt engineering as optional "tips & tricks" → ❌ misses that this is an entirely new skill
  • Skeptics treated as obstacles · Guardrails written after incidents · Success = "did they log in?"
Predicted outcome: Joins the 85% of enterprises stuck in pilot purgatory — every adaptive challenge met with a technical response.

The VALUE Framework Applied to AI Adoption

VALUE turns "do AI" into a question discipline — applied to the team we actually run.

V — Visualise

The 3–5 year picture: frontend/backend dissolve into full-stack engineers across dev, QA, DevOps & data. Software gets dramatically cheaper. The differentiator becomes "do you understand the business?" — engineers as growth partners alongside PM.

A — Anticipate & Ask

Catch "pilot becomes permanent pilot." Name the unspoken question: "If AI writes 60% of code, what happens to the engineer's identity?" Anticipate the week 6–8 productivity-disillusionment wave.

L — Lead from Purpose

Reframe the cause: not an "efficiency mandate" (reads as headcount cut), but "engineers as growth partners — closer to the business outcome." Would the skeptic see themselves as beneficiary or casualty?

U — Understand Context

Translate per audience. Cut through the hype honestly — Gen AI is one tool, not all of AI; durable value sits in real ML on our own data. Speaking plainly is itself trust-building.

E — Enable, Energise & Engage

Publish guardrails (incl. ethics & security) before usage. Build the missing data literacy — invest in ML fundamentals, not just prompt tips. Name the bottleneck shift; invest in AI-assisted testing & CI/CD. Measure honestly. Engage PM into the practice every sprint.

Skip any letter and adoption stalls in a predictable place. VALUE tells you which letter you skipped.

The Trust Model: Psychological Safety in an Uncertain World

Engineers cannot adopt what they fear will replace them. Trust is the prerequisite, not the by-product.

Reliability — do what you say

Publish a Coding Agents Policy (incl. ethics & security) and stick to it. Don't pivot tooling every quarter — stabilise the framework. "No one loses their job because of AI" must hold if you say it.

Acceptance — don't change people

"AI is wrong half the time" — they're right (it hallucinates) and protecting their craft. "My role won't exist in two years" is not resistance; it's a rational read. Make space for the fear.

Openness — no surprises

Be honest about the hype and hallucination. Share what you don't know: "we'll design the role evolution with you, not for you." Brown Bags surface what didn't work.

Congruence — walk my talk

Leaders must use AI visibly — including struggling publicly. Bring imperfect AI drafts to co-edit. If your calendar shows no AI learning time, the gap is visible and trust collapses.

Capstone move: Co-author AI usage guidelines with the loudest skeptic in each squad. People defend what they build — replace conversion with co-authoring.

Three Levels of Listening: What Engineers Actually Fear

Most adoption conversations stop at Level 1. The real challenges only surface at Levels 2 and 3.

Level 0 — My assumptions

Our bias: "engineers resist because they're behind." Reality: the ask was poorly framed. We assumed productivity gains were obvious — they aren't.

Level 1 — What is stated

"AI hallucinates." · "I still have to test everything — not faster." · "Tools change monthly; can't keep up." The presenting problem, not the underlying one.

Level 2 — Feelings, needs, fears

"Will my manager think I didn't do the work?" · "15 years mastering Java — what was it for?" (identity grief) · "PR queue is the same — it doesn't feel faster." Invisible at Level 1; the real blockers.

Level 3 — What is possible

"If you owned the AI quality guidelines, what would they look like?" · "If we freed up 40% of coding time, what would you do?" These questions turned skeptics into authors.

The Coding Agents Policy, the disclosure norms, the Brown Bag forum — all came from Level 2 + 3 listening, not a leadership decree.

Non-Linear Thinking (4 A's): The Weekly Cadence

Adoption is not an event you launch — it is a cadence you run.

Ask

What is each pilot owner not telling us in the status? Curious inquiry over status-grading. "Where is the bottleneck now?" — name it before it normalises.

Anticipate

Which pilots hit identity-threat in 30 days? Which model update breaks workflows this quarter? Which use case carries ethics/security risk? Prepare for the disillusionment wave before it arrives.

Amplify

Spotlight agents shipped to production — but also amplify engineers building the new skill: prompt engineering, data literacy, PM partnership. Amplify the new identity, not just the tool.

Align

Align with sponsors before the decision. Align on what "productivity" means before dashboards. Align with HR on the career path before engineers ask.

The 4 A's keep you ahead of challenges that move faster than quarterly planning — and in AI, everything does.
