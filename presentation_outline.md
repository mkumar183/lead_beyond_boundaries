## Slide 2 — The Challenge: Why AI Adoption Is Hard
**Type:** Problem framing slide

**Headline:** *AI Adoption is not a tooling problem. It is an adaptive challenge.*

**Bullets:**
- Multiple coding agents available (Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot) — 9 products, dozens of squads, 4 product groups
- Senior engineers feel craft is threatened; juniors fear looking lazy; EMs fear delivery risk
- Industry data: 78% of enterprises run AI pilots; **under 15% reach production**
- Beliefs, identities, habits, and loyalties must shift — not just licenses provisioned
- The question is not *"which tool?"* — it is *"how do we mobilise people to change how they work?"*

**Principle anchor:** Heifetz — *"Adaptive challenges can only be addressed through changes in people's priorities, beliefs, habits, and loyalties."*

---

## Slide 3 — Adaptive vs Technical: Diagnosing the Real Problem
**Type:** Two-column comparison (Technical | Adaptive)

**Left column — If we treat AI Adoption as TECHNICAL:**
- Known solution: buy licenses, run training videos, set quotas
- Command compliance: "everyone uses Copilot by Q3"
- Measure: % engineers logged in, lines generated, license utilisation
- Follow tried-and-tested rollout playbooks

**Right column — But AI Adoption is ADAPTIVE:**
- Unknown solution: requires new patterns of behaviour, not new tools
- Mobilise discovery: engineers must shed entrenched ways
- Measure: rework reduction, defect quality, cycle time, identity shift
- Requires changing attitudes, values, deep-seated behaviours

**Take-home line:** Diagnosing this correctly *is* the first leadership move. Get this wrong and every subsequent move misfires.

## Slide 4 — How We Would Have Approached This *Without* the Training
**Type:** "Default mode" / Loyal Soldier slide (intentionally uncomfortable)

**Headline:** *The Loyal Soldier defaults — well-intentioned, but adaptive-blind.*

**Bullets (each tagged to a Loyal Soldier behaviour from the workbook):**
- **Respond to expectations** → Wait for the executive AI mandate; then cascade as a directive
- **Comply and appease** → Top-down rollout: "leadership wants AI used, so use it"
- **Communicate at task levels** → Status reports: "X% adoption, Y licenses provisioned"
- **Wait to be invited** → Each EM runs their own pilot; no shared learning, no anticipation
- Skeptics treated as *obstacles to overcome* — convert them or work around them
- Guardrails written *after* incidents; reactive, not anticipatory
- Success measured as throughput ("did they log in?"), not quality ("did rework drop?")

**Principle anchor:** Workbook *Loyal Soldier* defaults from page 5.

**Predicted outcome:** Joins the 85% of enterprises stuck in pilot purgatory.

## Slide 6 — The VALUE Framework Applied to AI Adoption
**Type:** Five-column or 5-row card layout — one per letter

**Headline:** *VALUE turns "do AI" into a question discipline.*

| Letter | Workbook anchor question | Move it produces for AI Adoption |
|---|---|---|
| **V — Visualise** | "If this succeeds beyond expectations, what is different 3–5 years from now?" | Connect every pilot to grower outcomes — not engineering hours saved |
| **A — Anticipate & Ask** | "What patterns are repeating that we are normalising? What question are we afraid to ask?" | Catch "pilot becomes permanent pilot." Name senior-engineer identity threat as an addressable risk |
| **L — Lead from Purpose** | "What is the cause I am serving beyond my role or title?" | Reframe: not an "efficiency mandate" (sounds like headcount cut) but "honour what these tools are for — better grower decisions" |
| **U — Understand Context** | "Am I speaking the language of my stakeholder?" | Translate "this LLM does X" into agronomist language: "this changes how a seed recommendation gets made" |
| **E — Enable, Energise, Engage** | "What needs to be removed, not added?" | Publish guardrails *before* pushing usage. Remove the "is AI OK here?" ambiguity that blocks engineers |

**Take-home line:** VALUE is not a slogan; it is a discipline. Each letter has changed a specific decision we would have made differently.

## Slide 7 — The Trust Model: Why Skeptics Become Co-Authors
**Type:** Four-quadrant card layout (R · A · O · C)

**Headline:** *The skeptic is the signal, not the obstacle.*

**Four quadrants — Trust element → AI Adoption move:**

- **Reliability** — *Do what you say.* Publish a Coding Agents Policy and stick to it. Don't pivot tooling every quarter. Engineers trust the framework only if it holds.
- **Acceptance** — *Don't try to change people.* When a senior engineer says "AI gives wrong suggestions half the time," do not argue. Accept the position — it protects years of invested craft. Then move it.
- **Openness** — *People can take good news or bad news, but not surprises.* Brown Bag sessions surface what didn't work, not just success stories. Failure content is the trust-builder.
- **Congruence** — *Walk my talk.* Leaders who advocate AI must use it visibly themselves. Bring imperfect AI-drafted artefacts to be co-edited, not polished decks to be approved.

**Capstone move:** Co-author AI usage guidelines *with the loudest skeptic in each squad.* People defend what they build — conversion attempts replaced with co-authoring.

## Slide 8 — Three Levels of Listening: Hearing What Engineers Actually Fear
**Type:** Four-card stack (Level 0 → 1 → 2 → 3)

**Headline:** *Most adoption-discovery conversations stop at Level 1. The unlocks are at Levels 2 and 3.*

**Four levels — what changed when we listened deeper:**

- **Level 0 — My assumptions.** *Caught in ourselves:* "engineers will resist because they're behind on AI." That assumption was a bias, not a fact.
- **Level 1 — What is stated.** Engineers said: "AI hallucinates. Quality is risky." Useful, but the surface.
- **Level 2 — Feelings, needs, assumptions.** What they actually feared, when asked deliberately: *"Will my manager think I didn't do the work?"* That fear is invisible at Level 1. It is also the real blocker.
- **Level 3 — What is possible.** *"If you owned the AI quality guidelines, what would they look like?"* That single Level 3 question converted skeptics into authors.

**Take-home line:** Out of Level 2 + Level 3 listening came the Coding Agents Policy, AI usage disclosure norms, and the Brown Bag forum — not from a leadership decree.

## Slide 9 — Hofstede: Designing for the Culture We Actually Have
**Type:** Cultural-dimension cards mapped to design choices

**Headline:** *We work in a high-power-distance, high-uncertainty-avoidance, collectivist culture. Design for it, don't fight it.*

**Three dimensions, three design moves:**

- **High Power Distance** → Identify the *Invisible Influencer* in each squad — the senior engineer whose quiet adoption is the unlock. Co-author with them *before* any town hall. Status comes from rank — use that, don't pretend it doesn't exist.
- **High Uncertainty Avoidance** → Engineers will not experiment without guardrails. Publish the Coding Agents Policy *first*; only then push usage. Removing ambiguity is the largest single adoption unblocker.
- **Collectivism** → Adoption spreads through the squad, not through the individual. Brown Bag forum (cross-squad learning) is the natural shape; one-on-one champion programs are not.

**Take-home line:** Cultural dimensions are not obstacles — they are the operating system. Build for it.

## Slide 10 — Stakeholder Map + Non-Linear Thinking: The Operating Cadence
**Type:** Two-column — Stakeholder Map (left) | 4 A's (right)

**Left — Stakeholder Map applied:**
- Draw the map *before* any town hall
- **Trust line thickness** identifies which seniors will defend the change publicly
- **Reciprocity arrows** reveal where we ask too much and offer too little (often: pilot owners burning out)
- Re-draw quarterly. Thin spots are where to invest deliberately

**Right — Non-Linear Thinking (4 A's) as weekly practice:**
- **Ask** — What is each pilot owner *not* telling us in the weekly status? Curious inquiry over status-grading
- **Anticipate** — Build the radar: which pilots will hit identity-threat resistance in the next 30 days? Act now
- **Amplify** — Spotlight the two agents that shipped to production. Don't dilute attention by chasing every candidate use case
- **Align** — Have alignment conversations with sponsors *before* the moment of decision, not in the meeting itself

**Take-home line:** This is not a one-off design — it is a weekly cadence. The training shifts you from event-mode to practice-mode.

## Slide 11 — The Playbook: From Principles to Practice
**Type:** Synthesis slide — the playbook as a numbered sequence

**Headline:** *The AI Adoption Playbook — derived directly from the workbook.*

**The 8-step playbook:**

1. **Diagnose adaptive, not technical** *(Heifetz)* — name what kind of problem you are solving before you solve it.
2. **Take the Growth Partner stance** *(Mindshift)* — anticipate, create, communicate impact, influence by design.
3. **Run VALUE on every pilot** *(VALUE)* — five questions before any rollout move.
4. **Publish guardrails first** *(VALUE-E + Hofstede UAI)* — remove ambiguity *before* pushing usage.
5. **Co-author with skeptics** *(Trust-Acceptance)* — the loudest skeptic writes the boundary.
6. **Listen at Level 2 and 3** *(3 Levels)* — discover the fear behind the objection, then ask what is possible.
7. **Map and use Invisible Influencers** *(Stakeholder Map + Hofstede PDI)* — adoption spreads through them, not around them.
8. **Practice the 4 A's weekly** *(Non-Linear Thinking)* — Ask, Anticipate, Amplify, Align — as a cadence, not an event.

**Take-home line:** This is the playbook anyone in the org can pick up and run. It is principle-derived, use-case-tested, and squad-portable.

**Headline:** *Same goal. Different leadership choices.*

**Side-by-side close:**
| Without the training | With the training |
|---|---|
| Mandate tooling | Mobilise people |
| % logged in | Quality, rework, cycle time |
| Skeptics as obstacles | Skeptics as co-authors |

**Closing line:** *"The training did not change what we are trying to do. It changed how we are trying to do it. The playbook is principle-derived, use-case-tested, and ours to keep practising."*
