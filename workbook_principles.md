## 1. Adaptive vs Technical Challenges

Based on **Ronald Heifetz** (Harvard, leadership expert).

| Technical Challenges | Adaptive Challenges |
|---|---|
| Well defined and clear | Tangled, complex problems composed of multiple systems |
| Familiar problems / issues | Unique issues |
| **Known solution** — can be solved with knowledge and procedures already in hand | **Unknown solution** — cannot be resolved by expert knowledge and routine management alone |
| Involves knowledge, expertise and procedures (SOP) | Requires new / continuous learning, innovation, and new patterns of behaviour |
| Follow tried and tested success stories | Follow changing attitudes, values, and deep-seated behaviours |
| **Command** people to solve the issue | **Mobilise** people to solve the issue |

> *"Adaptive challenges can only be addressed through changes in people's priorities, beliefs, habits, and loyalties. Making progress requires going beyond any authoritative expertise to mobilise discovery, shedding certain entrenched ways, tolerating losses, and generating the new capacity to thrive anew."* — Ronald Heifetz

**Key takeaway:** 21st century leadership has adaptive challenges. Most organisational problems we face today cannot be solved by expert knowledge alone.

### Dance vs Balcony
- **Dance** — engaged in day-to-day activities.
- **Balcony** — higher view that allows us to awaken to our highest wisdom, potentials and vision.

A leader must move between both — engage in the work *and* step back to see patterns.

## 2. Mindset Shift: Loyal Soldier → Growth Partner

| FROM: Loyal Soldier | TO: Growth Partner |
|---|---|
| Respond to expectations | Anticipate & engage |
| Comply and appease | Create & collaborate |
| Communicate at task levels | Communicate impact |
| Wait to be invited | Influence by design |

This is the central behavioural shift the workshop demands.

## 3. The VALUE Framework

A question discipline for leading through adaptive challenges. Each letter is a deliberate question to ask before acting.

### **V — Visualise**
Think of the big picture. Look at the impact. See the long-term benefit. How will your actions today lead to long-term value creation? Imagine different stakeholders being joyful.

**Anchor questions:**
- If this succeeds beyond expectations, what is different 3–5 years from now?
- Who benefits first, who benefits most, and who benefits last?
- What value is created that does not show up on a dashboard?
- What has become easier, safer, faster, fairer, cheaper, more accessible?
- What would future leaders thank us for starting today?

### **A — Anticipate & Ask**
Notice the emergent patterns and anticipate the path forward. Read the field and the room and analyse for the hot spots. Ask for what will transform the situation.

**Anchor questions:**
- What patterns are repeating that we might be normalising?
- What questions are we afraid to ask? Why?
- Where is energy increasing, and where is it draining?
- What feels unspoken but influential in this situation?
- What question, if asked now, could meaningfully shift the trajectory?

### **L — Lead from Purpose**
Position yourself in a way that allows you to activate purpose. Purpose allows you to have a seat at the table and operate from a position of knowledge and cause.

**Anchor questions:**
- What is the cause I am serving beyond my role or title?
- What about this future success would excite me personally?
- Where am I reacting from position instead of acting from purpose?
- If I spoke from purpose, what would I say differently?
- What is the "compound interest" of the actions you are taking today?

### **U — Understand Context & Cultural Nuance**
Adapt and design communication based on cultural priorities. Work to build trust and impact.

**Anchor questions:**
- Am I speaking the language of my stakeholder? Or expecting them to learn mine?
- What does "success" mean in this culture, not in theory?
- What behaviours are rewarded here — even if unofficially?
- What would be respectful here that might look ineffective elsewhere?
- Where might my intent be correct but my impact misaligned?

### **E — Enable, Energise & Engage**
Value creation occurs through enablement of stakeholders and design for impact for their priorities.

**Anchor questions:**
- What is currently making it hard for others (stakeholders / end-users) to do their best work?
- Where is the friction? How can we resolve it best?
- What needs to be removed, not added?
- Apart from delivering what is needed, how can we deliver delight?

## 4. The Trust Model — Four Elements

Trust = Reliability + Acceptance + Openness + Congruence (with Integrity & Alignment as binding outcomes).

### **Reliability**
*Do what you say you will do.* Competence is a given.
- Take action, keep promises, meet deadlines, be on time
- Follow up — follow through; deliver
- Back promises with testimonials and evidence
- Recover from breakdowns effectively
- Reputation and price point

### **Acceptance**
*Accept the other person for who they are.* Don't judge, criticise, or make people feel inferior.
- Respect another's point of view; be empathetic
- Be non-judgmental about a person; listen
- Allow mistakes (and learn from them); accept limitations
- Don't try to change people; don't criticise
- Different = Different — understand cultural differences and amplify them
- Find similarities; appreciate the stretch out of comfort zone
- **Practices that create psychological safety through candour with respect**

### **Openness**
*Level with people. Don't hide things. Give the whole story.*
> "People can take good news or bad news, but they can't take surprises."
- Clarify expectations
- Be willing to hear what others say; be honest about limitations
- Be straightforward; data driven
- Tell people how you feel, what you think and believe
- Self-disclosure; curious inquiry
- Feedback — formal and informal; learning together
- Transparency around processes and challenges; thought partnership

### **Congruence**
*What you say is on track with what you believe and what you do.* Different from Reliability — Reliability is "I'll do what I say I'll do"; Congruence is saying and doing what you believe in the moment.
- Talk straight; don't play games; be sincere
- Set boundaries; make rules clear
- Walk my talk — head, heart, hands, words
- Don't be too kind
- Use body language that matches behaviour / talk
- Same message across teams
- Acknowledge the gap between intent and impact

## 5. Trusted Advisor & Power Sources

> **Trusted Advisor = Energy × Rank × Trust**

### Extrinsic Power Sources
- **Legitimate** — formal right to make demands; expects compliance
- **Reward** — ability to compensate another for compliance
- **Expert** — high level of skill and knowledge
- **Referent** — perceived attractiveness, worthiness, right to others' respect
- **Coercive** — ability to punish for non-compliance
- **Informational** — control over information others need

### Intrinsic Power Sources
- **Personal Power** — manifestation of energy, vision, ability to communicate, capacity to influence, emotional intelligence
- **Ideological Power** — comes from an ideal or vision
- **Soft Power** — influence others are wary of disrupting because it would impact their wellbeing (which is connected to yours)
- **Transcendent Power** — connection to something larger than oneself: creator/spirit, the natural world, wellbeing of the planet

## 6. Geert Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions

Six dimensions that describe how cultures differ — essential for the **U** (Understand Context) of VALUE.

### Power Distance (PDI)
Extent to which less powerful members accept and expect that power is distributed unequally.
- **Low PDI:** Value equality and power-levelling rituals; status connected to merit and ability.
- **High PDI:** Value hierarchy and role stature; status connected to rank & authority.

### Individualism vs Collectivism (IDV)
Extent to which people feel independent vs interdependent.
- **Individualism:** Individual needs over collective needs. Like atoms flying around in a gas.
- **Collectivism:** Collective needs over individual needs. Like atoms fixed in a crystal.

### Masculinity vs Femininity (MAS)
Extent to which the use of force is endorsed socially.
- **Masculinity:** Heroic focus on accomplishment. Winning is important; quantity matters; big is beautiful.
- **Femininity:** Cooperative focus on accomplishment. Sympathy for the underdog.

### Uncertainty Avoidance Index (UAI)
Society's tolerance for uncertainty and ambiguity (NOT risk avoidance, NOT rule following).
- **High UAI:** Anxiety in the face of the unknown; wish for fixed habits and rituals; clarity focus; avoidance of unfamiliar.
- **Low UAI:** Embracing the unfamiliar; shaping reality.

### Short-term vs Long-term Orientation (LTO)
- **Short-term:** Past provides moral compass; adhering to it is morally good. Focus on visible outcome; instant culture.
- **Long-term:** World is in flux; preparing for the future is always needed. Faith in invisible manifestation; continuity focus.

### Indulgence vs Restraint (IND)
- **Indulgence:** Good to be free; doing what your impulses want is good. Pleasure and materialism are integral to living.
- **Restraint:** Life is hard; duty (not freedom) is the normal state. Pleasure and materialism are distractions.

## 7. Three Levels of Listening

A discipline for deeper conversations with stakeholders, peers, and team members.

### **Level 0 — Listening to My Assumptions**
What is happening *inside you* — your own narrative, emotions, self-talk, biases, judgments.

**Anchor questions:**
- How am I feeling when I listen to my team member / peer?
- Is there a bias or judgment I am creating about them in this moment?
- To what degree am I fully present right now?

### **Level 1 — Listening to What Is Stated**
The words, facts, events, and story being presented. Tone, pace, repeated/emphasised words.

**Anchor questions:**
- What do you need right now? What is missing for you?
- What would you value most in this situation?
- What support do you feel you need right now?
- What are you asking for — from yourself, or others?

### **Level 2 — Listening to Feelings, Needs & Assumptions**
Tune in to what is *unspoken* — emotions, needs, underlying beliefs, body language, energy shifts.

**Anchor questions:**
- I noticed a pause when you mentioned that — what came up for you?
- What might you not be saying out loud yet?
- What are the fears that are holding you back?
- Can you describe in an image or symbol what you are feeling right now?

### **Level 3 — Listening to What Is Possible**
Expanding the field of what is possible. Co-creating new insights and futures.

**Anchor questions:**
- What new possibilities are opening up as you share with me?
- If you were to act with the best solution in mind, what would you do differently?
- What might become possible if this challenge was a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity?
- What is the boldest step you can imagine taking from here?

## 8. Stakeholder Map

A drawable artefact for understanding your network of relationships.

**Step 1 — Place your people.** Position stakeholders by category. Place closer to "ME" if you know them well or interact frequently. Use triangles around each initial.

**Step 2 — Characterise your relationships.**
- **Trust:** Use a line to represent the degree to which you can count on them and be fully open and vulnerable. **Thicker line = more trust.**
- **Reciprocity:** Use arrows. Two-way line if reciprocity is equal; one-way if you give more or receive more.

**Use:** Identify Invisible Influencers, thin spots in your network, over-reliance, and where to invest deliberately.

## 9. Non-Linear Thinking — The 4 A's

Behaviours that allow you to practise non-linearity (essential for adaptive leadership).

### **1. ASK**
Ask with curiosity. Learn everything that is to learn. Overcome information asymmetry.
- How often and where do you do this?
- Where do you need to start doing this more?

### **2. ANTICIPATE**
Build a radar system — internal (intuitive guidance) and external (network) — that allows you to anticipate disruptions, opportunities, and discontinuities.
- When have you listened to your gut and it helped you prepare?
- Who are great "information confidantes" who help you anticipate accurately?

### **3. AMPLIFY**
Focus on vision and purpose. Throw light and attention on what will get you ahead vs what doesn't seem to work or fixing things with minimal benefit. Manage risks with a forward-momentum appetite.
- Do you usually focus on what creates growth, or on what's missing from a perfect outcome?
- What is the implication of that focus?

### **4. ALIGN**
Know your tribe of non-linear pioneers and collaborate to create exceptional environments for success. Have alignment conversations *ahead of time* so partners are prepared, excited, and committed when the moment is right.
- Who do you need to ensure you move ahead well? What is your trust equation with them?
- Who do you need to include and energise into your circle of influence?

**The core thesis:** Most leadership challenges today are *adaptive*, not technical. You cannot command your way through them — you must mobilise people. The VALUE framework gives you the questions; the Trust Model gives you the relationships; the Three Levels of Listening give you the depth; the Stakeholder Map gives you the network; and Non-Linear Thinking gives you the practice. Together they shift you from Loyal Soldier (compliance) to Growth Partner (anticipation and design).
